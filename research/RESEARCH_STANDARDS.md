# Research, verification, and publication standards

## Finding statuses

- **Source collected:** document or link obtained but not analyzed
- **Hypothesis:** a possible explanation or risk requiring evidence
- **Needs verification:** initial evidence exists, but key facts or context missing
- **Verified finding:** supported by authoritative records and independently checked
- **Closed / corrected:** disproven, clarified, resolved, or superseded, with history retained

## Required fields for every proposed finding

1. Specific claim or observation
2. Time period and jurisdiction
3. Original source link(s), page numbers, meeting timestamps, or exact rows
4. Calculation method, units, and comparison base (if quantitative)
5. Material uncertainty and plausible alternative explanations
6. Additional records or verification required
7. Relevant agency's response, when requested and received
8. Potential impact (cost, service level, legal/regulatory, public trust)
9. Finding status and date reviewed

## Privacy / fairness

Only include employee or contractor names where relevant to documented public roles or decisions. Do not disclose protected personal identifiers, private addresses, personal phone numbers, bank details, or unrelated family information. Separate public financial transactions from accusations. A conflict-of-interest concern requires more than a shared surname, acquaintance, or campaign contribution.

Before publishing a serious allegation involving a person or company, verify with primary records, seek their response, and consider review by a qualified attorney or experienced journalist.

## Data quality checks

- Approved budget is not the same as actual spending.
- Appropriations and transfers are not necessarily cash payments.
- Expenditures may appear in multiple documents; avoid double-counting.
- Account for fiscal-year boundaries, fund type, inflation, population, service level, and one-time costs.
- Flag missing enterprise-fund and school-district data explicitly.
- Never manufacture missing amounts, vendor identities, or exact meeting votes.

## AI use

AI can assist extraction, categorization, search, and drafting, but must not become the sole source for factual claims. Verify original PDF/CSV rows, meeting audio, or official minutes before publication. Maintain a human-reviewed audit trail.
