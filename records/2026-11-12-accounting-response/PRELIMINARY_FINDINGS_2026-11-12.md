# Preliminary expenditure observations — Dartmouth accounting exports

**Review date:** November 12, 2026  
**Status:** Preliminary calculations from six Town-supplied accounting exports. **Not independently reconciled to official ledgers, cash disbursements, or audited statements.**

**Source files:** Described in [the intake README](README.md), with SHA-256 checksums in [the manifest](source-manifest.csv). Full workbooks are withheld from this public repository pending privacy review. This file publishes aggregate observations and supplier names only.

## 1. Water Enterprise Fund — invoices to City of New Bedford

Source workbook: `2022-2027 WEF SEF Invoice Listing.xlsx`, filtered to `ORG DESC = W.E.F.-Expenses` and `VENDOR NAME = CITY OF NEW BEDFORD`. Signed `AMOUNT` totals:

| Fiscal year | Number of matching entries | Amount |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| FY2022 | 23 | $1,212,625.60 |
| FY2023 | 23 | $1,810,395.82 |
| FY2024 | 23 | $1,090,889.54 |
| FY2025 | 23 | $1,954,256.93 |
| FY2026 | 25 | $2,393,051.25 |

FY2026 is **97.3%** above FY2022 and $438,794.32 above FY2025 in these particular operating invoices. The full `W.E.F.-Expenses` set in FY2026 totals $3,991,016.93, so City of New Bedford entries constitute about **60%** of this category's invoice amounts. Do **not** confuse that ratio with percentage of all municipal spending, total water budget, or water consumption.

FY2027 is partial and includes transactions dated through November 2026; **do not extrapolate annual totals** from it.

### Relevant context
- Dartmouth's February 2026 announcement says several wells had been offline since a 2020 storm, causing increased water purchases from New Bedford and contributing to a mid-year 13% rate increase: https://dartmouthma.gov/m/NewsFlash/Home/Detail/655
- Dartmouth's September 18, 2025 Finance Committee minutes state historically about 20% of water came from New Bedford, but in the prior year the share was about 50%: https://www.dartmouthma.gov/AgendaCenter/ViewFile/Minutes/_09182025-5111
- The 2026 Annual Town Report documents a **$2,594,600** contract with Dankris Builders for upgrades at the 579 Old Westport Road treatment facility, scheduled for completion in July 2026: https://www.dartmouthma.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/426
- Public reporting of July 2026 meetings suggests the Panelli wells had resumed pumping but still had equipment problems; **verify against original minutes/video and operational records** before relying on meeting transcript alone.

### Questions
1. How many gallons were bought each month from New Bedford FY2022–FY2027, and what was the contractual cost per 1,000 gallons?
2. Which wells were operating each month and at what volume? When were repairs completed?
3. How much purchased-water cost is avoidable after plant upgrades, net of pumping/treatment/maintenance costs?
4. What were actual FY2027 monthly bills and whether any future-dated entries are advance billings?

## 4. Solid Waste Enterprise Fund — capital effect on annual comparisons

Source: `2022-2027 SWEF Invoice Listing.xlsx`.

- FY2025 signed entries: **$1,909,758.16**.
- FY2026 signed entries: **$1,316,332.14** (apparent **31.1% drop**).
- FY2025 includes two `S.W.E.F.-Articles` entries to `BALLARD MACK SALES` of **$448,000** and **$265,000** (total **$713,000**); one explicitly says `2025 MACK TRUCK`.

Excluding those two FY2025 amounts yields $1,196,758.16, making FY2026's exported invoices about **10% higher** rather than lower. This is a **like-for-like adjustment illustration**, not a formal normalization or proof there were no other nonrecurring items.

Follow-up: fleet capital plan, actual asset count, maintenance history, useful lives, disposal proceeds, and replacement approvals. The truck count is **not** established from the two invoice entries.

## 6. DPW fleet and road-program transactions worth tracing

- FY2026 general-fund entries to `BOSTON FREIGHTLINER` total **$564,950** across three entries coded to `DPW Highway Articles` and `Articles-Construction Division`. Two descriptions reference a municipal purchasing contract; do not assert exact truck or vehicle count without purchase orders.
- FY2026 `Other Funds` entries to `P.J. KEATING COMPANY` total approximately **$1.797M**, across the Highway Improvement Fund, `CH.90 Fair Share Program`, and `NSTAR Gas-Sharp St:Gift Acnt`. Similar vendor entries in the General Fund may represent separate portions of shared road projects; **do not double-count project costs** without matching invoice/contract documents.
- Ask for equipment and fixed-asset inventory, five-year fleet replacement plan, bid/award records and completed road-project details. Compare costs against maintained roadway miles and outputs.

## Reconciliation and data-quality rules

- Invoice `AMOUNT` is not necessarily cash actually paid on the invoice date.
- `INV`, `CRM`, `DIR`, positive and negative rows may reflect different accounting activities.
- Encumbrance-coded lines and vendor payments can overlap accounting phases, and the General Fund / Other Funds / enterprise exports might overlap on shared projects.
- Both files titled "2022-2026" contain FY2027 entries; FY2027 is incomplete.
- The export's fields do **not** show employee counts, hours, gallons of water bought, miles of roads maintained, or service quality.
- Distinguish operating costs from capital projects, one-time grants and transfers.
- None of the patterns above is itself evidence of misuse of public funds.

## Recommended first three targeted follow-ups

1. **Water:** monthly purchased-water gallons, cost, supplier contracts/rate sheets, well uptime, and project completion reports.
2. **DPW:** snowfall-adjusted snow/ice spending and quantities, contractor unit rates, fleet inventory/utilization, and work-order performance.
3. **School buses:** bid tabulations, school transportation contracts and route/service breakdown for FY2022–FY2027.

Do not issue a new broad records request until the Town has responded to the existing staffing/payroll request and accounting exports have been reconciled.
